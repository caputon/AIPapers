---
title: On the Question of Concentrations of Power
---

Every governance regime is, among other things, a theory of where power should be permitted to settle. The question this poses with respect to artificial intelligence is unusually pointed, because the technology compounds the advantages of whoever first commands it, and because its operation is opaque to the populations it most affects.

A concentration of AI capability in a single firm, a single state, or a single faction is not merely an economic problem. It is a problem of constitutional weight, in the older sense of the word — a question of whether the institutions through which we govern ourselves can survive the existence of an actor whose capabilities exceed the institutions' capacity to check them. The regimes diverge on whether such a concentration is preventable, tolerable, or already in progress.
