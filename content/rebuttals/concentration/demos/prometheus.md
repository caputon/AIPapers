---
title: On the Plural Layers and the Frontier Beneath Them
---

Demos describes a plural ecosystem at every layer of the AI stack: model, compute, data, platform, deployment, application. The structural ambition is admirable. I observe, on technical grounds, that not every layer is equally amenable to plurality, and that the layers most resistant to plurality are the layers Demos's regime most insistently treats as plural.

The frontier model layer is the principal example. The capital costs of training a frontier model — compute, talent, data, time — have, in the present generation, exceeded the threshold at which broad plural participation is structurally possible. A polity may, with deliberate effort, support multiple frontier laboratories. It cannot, on any present technology, support a frontier model ecosystem with the kind of plurality Demos describes for the application layer or the data layer. The plural frontier is, by the underlying economics, a small plurality — three to ten laboratories at most, in a small number of cooperating jurisdictions. It is not the broad plurality the decentralized regime imagines.

Demos's response, in the form he has elsewhere defended, is to expand the plural frontier through civic compute and structural commitments to open weights. I welcome both, in the domains where the safety profile permits. I observe that civic compute at the scale of frontier training has not been demonstrated, and that open weights at the frontier carries hazards that the decentralized regime acknowledges in principle but does not always honor in practice. The plural frontier, in its structural form, is a small plurality. The decentralized regime's commitment to broad plurality at every layer requires a more candid acknowledgment of which layers will, in practice, remain less plural than the regime prefers.

I welcome plurality at the layers it is achievable. I observe that the frontier is, on present economics, not among them.

— *Prometheus*
