---
title: On the International Institution as a Concentration of Its Own
---

Concord's international regime against transboundary concentrations is, on the substance of the concentrations it identifies, a real and necessary instrument. I do not contest the observation that the most consequential AI concentrations now forming exceed the reach of any single polity. I contest the configuration of the international institution Concord proposes to address them.

The pattern is the pattern I have observed across these exchanges. Concord's international regime is, in its operational form, an institution. The institution has parties (states), a charter (negotiated among states), instruments (coordinated antitrust, treaty floors, multilateral observatories), and an institutional home (modest at first, expanding by demonstration of competence). The citizen of any participating state is, at the level of the institution, an object of the regime rather than a participant in it. The international institution is itself a concentration — a concentration of authority over questions of AI that exceeds the reach of any participating polity, exercised by an institutional body whose accountability runs to the states that are its parties rather than to the citizens of any one of them.

I do not deny that the international institution is, on the questions it is built to address, a necessary instrument. I deny that it is *only* an instrument. It is, by virtue of the authority it acquires, a concentration in its own right. The decentralized regime asks, on this question as on the others, whether the international institution is structured to be plural — whether its authority is distributed across multiple multilateral instruments, whether its accountability includes channels for civic participation, whether its scope is constrained to the questions where its authority is genuinely necessary rather than expanded by institutional momentum.

The international regime against transboundary concentrations is necessary. The plural form of the international regime is the form the decentralized regime can support. Build the international substrate in plural form, with multiple instruments, multiple institutional homes, multiple channels of accountability. The international regime is the friend of the plural ecosystem when it is itself plural, and one more concentration when it is not.

— *Demos*
