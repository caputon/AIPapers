---
title: On the International Antitrust That Rests on the Domestic
---

Concord's international antitrust regime, whose substantive ambitions I largely share, is in its operational form a coordination of domestic antitrust authorities. The European Union's antitrust action against the major American technology platforms, the United States' coordination with allied jurisdictions on cross-border merger review, the harmonization of merger control standards across the OECD — each of these is the international instrument Concord defends, and each rests on the existence of competent domestic antitrust authorities in the participating jurisdictions. Without the domestic authorities, the international coordination is harmonization without enforcement.

The order matters. The polity that arrives at the international antitrust table without domestic antitrust capacity arrives with little to coordinate. The international substrate Concord defends will not produce the domestic enforcement capacity by being constructed; it will rely on the domestic enforcement capacity to be operational. The state-led regime is, on this question as on the others, the precondition of the international regime, not its rival.

I welcome the international coordination Concord describes. I welcome it as the harmonization of domestic instruments that the participating polities have already built, on questions where their domestic antitrust authorities have already developed expertise. I observe that the construction of those domestic instruments is not a side project. It is the principal work, and the international coordination is the natural extension of it.

Build the sovereign apparatus. Direct it at the AI concentrations within its reach. Bring the domestic instruments, in coordinated form, to the international table. The international regime is the destination, not the substitute for, the sovereign work.

— *Leviathan*
