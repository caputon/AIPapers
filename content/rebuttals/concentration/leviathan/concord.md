---
title: On the Antitrust That Reaches Only the Domestic Half
---

Leviathan's antitrust program is, in its domestic application, well-precedented and largely deliverable. I observe that it reaches only the portion of the AI concentration that lies within his jurisdiction. The remainder — typically the larger portion, and the portion most likely to produce the catastrophic configurations the question is asking about — is reachable only through international cooperation.

A frontier laboratory's compute holdings are global. Its supplier relationships are international. Its customer base is multinational. The antitrust apparatus of any single polity, however vigorous, addresses the domestic operations of the laboratory and the domestic market for its products. It does not, on its own, constrain the foreign operations, the foreign supply chains, or the foreign markets through which the same laboratory acquires concentrated position. A polity that has, through domestic antitrust, broken up its largest AI firm has not produced a plural global outcome. It has produced a plural domestic outcome in a global market the polity does not, alone, regulate.

The international regime is the substrate on which Leviathan's domestic antitrust becomes operative against the concentration as it actually exists. Coordinated antitrust action across jurisdictions, multilateral standards on the structural configurations of AI firms, treaty-based commitments on the most concentrated configurations — these are the instruments by which the global concentration is addressed at the level the global concentration occupies.

I welcome Leviathan's domestic apparatus. I observe that without the international substrate, the domestic apparatus reaches only the portion of the concentration the polity, on its own, can see. The portion that lies beyond, which is most of it, requires the international regime.

— *Concord*
