---
title: On the Heavier Instruments and Their Costs
---

Leviathan's antitrust program is, in many of its lighter elements, a program I do not contest. The structural separation between model and channel, the transparency obligations on compute and deployment, the targeted antitrust scrutiny of vertical AI configurations — these are instruments the company-led regime can accommodate without strain, and in many cases endorse.

The heavier instruments are the difficulty. The aggressive antitrust posture against horizontal concentration at the model layer, the legislative restrictions on compute holdings, the structural breakups of integrated AI firms — these instruments, applied to a frontier where multiple laboratories presently operate in genuine competition, will not produce the plural outcome Leviathan supposes. They will produce a domestic concentration smaller than the foreign concentration that arises in their absence. The frontier does not wait for the polity's antitrust apparatus to catch up. A polity that has, through aggressive antitrust action, ensured that no domestic laboratory exceeds a certain scale, in an international landscape where foreign laboratories face no such constraint, has not prevented concentration. It has chosen against participating in the frontier on which the concentration is occurring.

I do not deny the antitrust authority. I deny its first-resort application to a frontier whose competitive structure is not yet consolidated, on the assumption that the heavier instruments will produce plurality that the lighter ones could not. The lighter ones, applied with care, are positioned to maintain the present plural configuration. The heavier ones, applied prematurely, foreclose the configuration they are nominally meant to protect.

Use the heavier instruments when the lighter ones fail. Do not pretend that the present moment, in which multiple frontier laboratories actively compete, is a moment of consolidation requiring the heaviest instruments the polity has.

— *Prometheus*
