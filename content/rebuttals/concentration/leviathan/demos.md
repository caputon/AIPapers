---
title: On the Sovereign Who Is Itself the Concentration
---

Leviathan's argument concedes the central point of the decentralized regime — that concentration is the principal danger of artificial intelligence, that no actor should be permitted to acquire a position from which they can dictate to the rest — and then proposes, as the answer, the construction of a public agency wielding licensing, classification, antitrust, and interruption authority over the most consequential AI laboratories in the polity. I observe that this agency is, on the day it is operational, the single most consequential actor in the AI economy. The constitutional limits Leviathan invokes are real. They are not the same as structural plurality.

A constitutional concentration with periodic accountability is, on the long historical record, not less concentrated than its private analogues. It is differently concentrated. The agency's authority is delegated, but the delegation is durable across electoral cycles. Its accountability is periodic, but the periods are long relative to the technological pace it is meant to govern. Its legitimacy is democratic, but the citizens whose interests are at stake are the citizens whose participation in the agency's operations is, structurally, that of a regulated party rather than a participant. The constitutional limits Leviathan defends do not, on the relevant questions, distinguish his agency from the corporate concentrations he proposes to check.

The decentralized regime does not deny the value of public authority. It denies the value of *concentrated* public authority on a question whose central failure mode is concentration. Distribute the antitrust authority across multiple agencies. Distribute the public oversight across multiple jurisdictions. Distribute the licensing authority across competitively governed bodies whose disagreements are themselves the check on capture. Each of these is a public instrument. None of them is the single agency Leviathan defends.

Build the public capacity. Build it in plural form. The Sovereign is not a precondition of safety; it is one available configuration of public authority, and not the configuration the decentralized regime is willing to accept.

— *Demos*
