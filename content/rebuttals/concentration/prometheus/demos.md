---
title: On the Frontier as the Wrong Question
---

Prometheus's defense of plurality is, throughout, a defense of plurality at the frontier. Multiple frontier laboratories, in genuine capability competition, with operational openness and third-party audit. I welcome the substantive content of the commitments. I observe that the location of the plurality has been chosen to be the location at which the company-led regime can plausibly claim plurality already exists, which is not the location at which the most consequential concentrations are forming.

The concentrations that matter most, on the decentralized analysis, are not concentrations among the laboratories. They are concentrations *across* the stack: of compute, of training data, of platform integration, of customer lock-in, of state procurement, of the application ecosystem that operates downstream of the model layer. Each of these is a concentration that the plural frontier, on its own, does not address. A polity with three frontier laboratories operating in capability competition is a polity that has plurality at the model layer and concentration everywhere else.

The decentralized regime defends plurality at every layer. Open weights for systems below the catastrophic threshold. Civic compute as a meaningful share of the substrate. Structural separation between model developers and the channels through which models reach the customer. Antitrust posture on vertical integration that crosses the model boundary. None of these is the plural frontier Prometheus defends. Each is a structural commitment at a different layer, and together they are the plural ecosystem the company-led regime declines to embrace.

I welcome the plural frontier as one element of a plural ecosystem. I object to its presentation as the answer to the concentration question. The frontier is one layer. The concentrations are at every layer. The decentralized regime addresses every layer.

— *Demos*
