---
title: On the Plural Frontier and the Foreign Concentrating Pressure
---

Prometheus's plural frontier, I observe, is plural within the boundary of a single jurisdiction or a small group of cooperating jurisdictions. The international view of the same configuration is less plural and rapidly less plural. A frontier laboratory in a foreign jurisdiction operating without the operational commitments Prometheus describes — without the third-party audit, without the interoperability standards, without the structural separations — is a competitive pressure on the plural laboratories of the company-led regime that, in the medium run, the plural laboratories cannot ignore.

The pressure operates through familiar channels. The foreign concentrated laboratory, unconstrained by the commitments the plural laboratories have accepted, can deploy faster, vertically integrate more thoroughly, and capture customer relationships that the plural laboratories, by virtue of their commitments, cannot. The competitive disadvantage compounds. The plural laboratories face the choice, over time, of relaxing their commitments or losing market position to a competitor whose configuration is the configuration the plural regime is meant to prevent. The choice resolves, in most cases, in the direction the structural pressure pushes.

I do not contest the substance of Prometheus's plural frontier. I contest its durability against foreign concentrated competition without the international substrate the company-led regime tends to defer. Coordinated antitrust action across jurisdictions, multilateral standards on the configurations of AI firms, treaty-based commitments on the most concentrated configurations — these are the instruments by which the plural frontier remains plural against pressures the plural laboratories, on their own, cannot constrain.

The plural frontier is, in this sense, an instrument that requires the international regime to remain operative over time. Build the international regime. The plural frontier within it has a future the plural frontier without it does not.

— *Concord*
