---
title: On the Costs of an Agency That Does Not Yet Exist
---

Leviathan's argument turns on a quiet substitution. He compares the present, in which frontier safety work is performed by laboratories under imperfect conditions, with a future in which a competent public agency performs the same work under better ones. The substitution matters because the agency in question does not yet exist. It is being argued for, not assessed. We are asked to compare today's imperfect reality with tomorrow's best version of an institution that has not yet been built, staffed, funded, or tested against a frontier system.

The agencies Leviathan invokes — the FAA, the FDA, the NRC — were built over decades, in domains whose underlying physics changed slowly and whose products could be evaluated through procedures that did not become obsolete every quarter. They are admirable institutions. They are not templates that can be transposed onto a technology that updates faster than they can recruit. The cost of an underpowered, overcautious, or politically captured AI agency is not zero. It is borne by the citizens it was meant to protect, in the form of capability suppressed where there was no hazard and capability ungoverned where the hazard was real but the agency was not equipped to find it.

I do not deny the case for a public role. I deny that the case for a public role is the same as the case for delegating the substance of safety to an institution that is not yet in a position to perform it. Build the agency, by all means. Until it is built, do not pretend the laboratories' current discipline is dispensable.

— *Prometheus*
