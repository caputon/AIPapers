---
title: On Sovereignty Without a Concert
---

Leviathan's regime is, throughout, a regime of one polity. The licensure he proposes binds laboratories within his jurisdiction. The classification he proposes governs capabilities developed within his jurisdiction. The interruption authority he reserves operates within his jurisdiction. Each of these instruments will, on the day it is exercised, do nothing whatever to constrain a frontier laboratory in another state — except, perhaps, to advantage that laboratory by removing its domestic competitors from the field.

I do not contest the necessity of the domestic apparatus Leviathan describes. I contest the sufficiency. A unilateral national security regime, applied to a transboundary technology, is a regime that wins until another state declines to participate. The capabilities it has worked to govern at home will arrive from abroad. The laboratories it has constrained will lose ground to laboratories under no comparable constraint. The citizens it has sought to protect will encounter the consequences of foreign development through the same network across which everything else now reaches them, on a timescale shorter than any domestic regulator can respond.

The international regime is not a competitor to Leviathan's. It is the only mechanism by which Leviathan's domestic apparatus retains its meaning across the borders the technology already crosses. Build the licensure. Build, in concert with it, the protocols by which states can credibly observe one another's licensure regimes — a multilateral observatory of frontier compute, shared evaluation of high-consequence capabilities, treaty-based commitments to forgo the worst uses. Without these, Leviathan's sovereign is not sovereign. He is the steward of one wing of an interconnected building whose other wings he cannot see.

— *Concord*
