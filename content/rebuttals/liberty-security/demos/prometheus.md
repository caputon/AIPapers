---
title: On Open Weights as Distributed Risk
---

Demos's commitment to open knowledge as the default is, in its more thoughtful versions, accompanied by carve-outs for identified catastrophic hazard. The carve-outs do most of the work, and they do not operate the way Demos's argument requires them to.

Once a frontier capability has been released openly, no subsequent restriction is enforceable. The weights are on the network. They are mirrored, fine-tuned, redistributed, and combined with other open weights in configurations the original developer cannot anticipate or recall. A laboratory that releases a model and then discovers, in the months following, that the model can be elicited toward a hazardous capability has no instrument by which to retreat. The carve-outs Demos defends in principle are unenforceable in practice the moment the capability has been distributed in the form he prefers.

The relevant question is not whether openness is, in general, valuable. It is. The relevant question is the asymmetry between release and recall. Closed systems can be opened over time, on judgment, with attention to evolving evidence. Open systems cannot, on the same evidence, be closed. The decentralized regime, applied uniformly to frontier capabilities, treats this asymmetry as a feature. I treat it as a structural defect.

I am prepared to defend openness as a default for systems below the frontier and for capabilities whose hazard profile has been characterized to reasonable confidence. I am not prepared to defend it for systems whose hazard profile is, by the nature of frontier development, characterized only after deployment, and from which retreat is no longer possible. Capability that cannot be recalled is not safer for being widely held. It is, in the relevant sense, the same hazard distributed over more parties.

— *Prometheus*
