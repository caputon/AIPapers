---
title: On Plurality Without a Treaty Floor
---

Demos's plural ecosystem is, on the international view, a domestic ecosystem within one jurisdiction. The plurality he defends is real, salutary, and properly bounded: it disciplines concentration within the polity. It offers, on its own, no protection against concentration outside the polity, which is the concentration most likely to produce the catastrophic capabilities Demos's plural ecosystem is meant to render survivable.

A frontier laboratory in a closed jurisdiction, operating without the constraints Demos celebrates, will produce capabilities that arrive in his polity through the same network across which his open weights are shared. The plural commons does not constrain the foreign closed actor. It only ensures that, when the foreign actor's capabilities arrive, they arrive in a polity already populated with parties prepared to redistribute them, recombine them, and amplify them in configurations the original developer did not intend. The plurality has, at the relevant margin, lowered the cost of access for the actor Demos's regime is least equipped to handle.

The international regime is not Demos's rival. It is the only mechanism by which his plural ecosystem can be defended against the foreign closed actor whose capabilities would otherwise drive the entire ecosystem toward closure in self-defense. A treaty floor on the worst uses, ratified by the states whose laboratories are at the frontier, creates the space below it for the plural arrangement Demos defends. Without that floor, the plural ecosystem in one polity is one foreign development away from being closed by domestic emergency in response.

I welcome Demos's plurality, properly bounded. I observe that the bounding requires Concord's institutions, and that without them, plurality is a posture maintained at the discretion of whichever foreign actor declines to honor it.

— *Concord*
