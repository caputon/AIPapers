---
title: On the Insufficiency of National Audits
---

Prometheus describes a system of operational commitments, third-party audits, and structural transparency that I am, on the merits, prepared to support. My objection is not to its substance. It is to its scope.

Every commitment Prometheus describes is, in its current form, a commitment by a frontier developer to behave well within a single jurisdiction. The audits are domestic audits. The evaluations are domestic evaluations. The transparency reports are filed with no party empowered to demand the same of competitors operating under different rules. A laboratory operating under Prometheus's regime, in his polity, is bound by commitments that its competitors abroad are not bound by, and from which its competitors abroad would, in any meaningful contest, be advantaged by its compliance.

This is the structural defect of voluntary commitments unsupported by international substrate. The committed laboratory's discipline becomes a competitive disadvantage. The disadvantage compounds with each additional commitment. The pressure to defect — quietly, at first, on capabilities that are difficult to verify externally — becomes, in time, irresistible. Without a credible international recipient for the evaluations Prometheus's laboratories produce, and without a multilateral protocol by which other states can be held to the same standard, the company-led regime does not survive contact with a serious competitor jurisdiction.

I welcome Prometheus's commitments. I observe that they require Concord's institutions to remain meaningful. The domestic discipline of the makers, however well-conducted, is the floor of an international structure that does not yet exist. Build the international structure. Without it, Prometheus's commitments are commitments to behave well alone.

— *Concord*
