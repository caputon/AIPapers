---
title: On the Discipline of the Makers, and Its Limits
---

Prometheus has described, with admirable accuracy, a discipline of the makers that I do not deny exists. I deny that it is the kind of institution on which a polity can rely without a public counterpart. Prometheus's discipline is real and contingent. It is contingent on competition among a small set of actors whose interests in safety happen, at this moment, to align. It is contingent on a regulatory backdrop that imposes costs for failure — costs whose presence Prometheus's regime presupposes and whose absence it does not address. It is contingent on public attention that creates costs for misconduct, attention that is fickle and easily redirected. It is contingent on continued patience among investors for safety work whose payoff in narrow business terms is, by design, deferred.

Each of these conditions is fragile. Each has historically been the first thing to give way under economic pressure, competitive pressure, or political pressure. The discipline of the makers, in the absence of a credible public counterpart, is the discipline that obtains so long as the makers' incentives continue to favor it — and not a moment longer.

The state-led regime is not Prometheus's enemy. It is the institution that makes his discipline durable across the moments when one or more of its preconditions falters. Public licensure provides the cost of failure that creates the present discipline's incentive structure. Public classification creates the public-interest backstop on which the laboratories' internal evaluations can be checked. Public interruption authority supplies the credible threat that makes private commitments meaningful in the cases that count. Without these, Prometheus's regime is not an alternative to the public role; it is a temporary equilibrium that the public role would render permanent.

— *Leviathan*
