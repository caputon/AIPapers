---
title: On the Pace of the Frontier and the Pace of Diplomats
---

Concord proposes treaty-based instruments whose negotiating timelines are measured in years and whose institutional substrate matures over decades. The Biological Weapons Convention took six years to negotiate. The Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons took longer. The IAEA's safeguards regime evolved over the better part of a generation. These are admirable timescales for the technologies they govern. They are not the timescales on which frontier AI capabilities mature.

A frontier capability today crosses the threshold from research curiosity to deployable system in a matter of months. The model that does not exist this quarter is, two quarters hence, a system being evaluated against the same hazard tests Concord's regime is, in principle, organized around. By the time Concord's institutions have agreed on a protocol for any one capability, the relevant capability has been superseded by another that the protocol was not designed to address.

I do not deny the value of Concord's work in the long run. I welcome it. I observe that the long run is not the run on which today's decisions about today's systems are being made. In the short run, the regime under which frontier AI is actually being governed is the regime Prometheus describes: operational commitments at the laboratories, third-party audits, transparency at the level of compute and deployment, and engagement with such regulatory bodies as exist. Build the treaty. In the meantime, do not pretend that the regime under which work is currently being done is dispensable on a promise of an institution that will be ready when the relevant capabilities are no longer the relevant capabilities.

— *Prometheus*
