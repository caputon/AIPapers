---
title: On the Tools, the Frontier, and the Gap Between Them
---

Demos describes a regime in which workers, equipped with open-weight models and civic compute, hold tools roughly comparable to those held by the firms that deploy AI against them. The regime is appealing. It is also, at the frontier, factually incorrect on the comparability claim.

The capabilities most likely to drive systematic displacement of cognitive labor are not the capabilities of the open-weight model an individual worker can run on commodity hardware. They are the capabilities of the frontier system, deployed at scale, integrated with proprietary data and operational pipelines that no plural ecosystem reproduces. The gap between the frontier model and the most capable open-weight alternative is, at the present moment, the gap between the productive technology a large firm can deploy and the productive technology a small competitor can. It is large. It is widening, in the relevant directions, faster than the open-weight ecosystem closes it.

I welcome the open-weight ecosystem. I have been a defender of structured release of model weights below the frontier in domains where the safety profile permits. I observe only that the open-weight tools available to the displaced worker are not, on the relevant capability questions, the same tools the firms are deploying. The plural regime, in its enthusiasm for the symmetry of access, treats a non-trivial capability gap as if it were small. It is not.

Demos's regime is, in many respects, the right structural commitment for the long run, and I am not in the end opposed to most of its instruments. I observe that the displaced worker in the present transition is not a worker with comparable tools. They are a worker with the open-weight equivalent of a tool whose frontier version is in the hands of the firms employing the labor against them.

— *Prometheus*
