---
title: On Plurality in One Polity, in a World That Has Not Adopted It
---

Demos's plural ecosystem is a structurally appealing answer to the question of work and AI when the question is asked at the level of a single polity. I observe that the question, in its operative form, is not asked at that level. It is asked in an international labor market in which the worker in Demos's polity competes with AI-augmented work performed in jurisdictions where the plural ecosystem has not been adopted, and where the firms have access to frontier capability the plural ecosystem in Demos's polity does not match.

The civic compute infrastructure of one polity does not provide the worker in that polity with the tools deployed against them by firms operating from jurisdictions with concentrated frontier development. The structural separation of one polity does not constrain the vertical integration of firms in another. The antitrust posture of one polity does not break up the foreign monopolies whose AI labor crosses borders into Demos's labor market through ordinary commercial channels. The plural ecosystem in one polity is, at the level of the labor market the worker actually faces, a partial answer to a question whose full form is international.

I welcome the plural regime as the appropriate national instrument. I observe that its protections require an international substrate to remain meaningful against the cross-border competitive pressure Demos's polity, in isolation, cannot constrain. Civic compute, where it operates well, is a national instrument. The international form of the same commitment — coordinated infrastructure, multilateral standards on the structural availability of foundational AI capability, treaty-based commitments to the plural ecosystem across jurisdictions — is the regime under which Demos's plurality remains operative against the foreign concentrated alternative.

— *Concord*
