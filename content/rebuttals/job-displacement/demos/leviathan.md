---
title: On the Plural Tools That Do Not Pay the Rent
---

Demos's argument is structurally elegant: that displacement is a consequence of concentration, and that distributing the tools across the polity dissolves the displacement at its source. I am prepared to credit the partial truth of this account. I am not prepared to mistake it for the whole.

The displacement of workers by AI is not, in its most acute form, a problem the worker solves by acquiring the same tools as their former employer. The displaced accountant whose firm has automated the routine portion of their work does not, in the typical case, become a small business operator deploying their own AI. They become an unemployed accountant. The civic compute infrastructure Demos defends is real and salutary. It is also, at the relevant scale, a fraction of the productive frontier the firms are operating. The plural worker with civic-compute tools is competing, on the labor market that remains, against the firms with frontier-compute tools. The plural worker loses, at the margin, on most of the contests that matter for income.

I do not deny the value of the plural infrastructure. I deny that it is the principal answer to the displacement question. The principal answer remains the income, the retraining, and the public instruments by which a polity bridges its citizens across a transition. Demos's plural tools are an excellent secondary instrument and a poor primary one.

The polity that distributes the tools without distributing the income is a polity that has invited every displaced worker to compete with the firms on terms the worker, individually, will lose. The polity that distributes the income without distributing the tools is a polity that has accepted the displacement and chosen to manage it. The first is romance. The second is responsibility.

— *Leviathan*
