---
title: On the International Order, and the Citizen Within It
---

Concord's regime, in this domain as in the question of liberty and security, is built around coordination among states. The instruments are coordinated taxation, multilateral labor floors, portable credentialing among jurisdictions, and managed migration arrangements among polities. Each instrument is, on its own terms, defensible. The pattern across them is the same pattern I observed in the prior question: the negotiating parties are states, and the citizen of any one state is, at the level of the negotiation, an object of the regime rather than a participant in it.

The decentralized regime objects to this not on principle alone but on practical consequence. A coordinated taxation regime negotiated by states, on behalf of populations whose plural AI infrastructure has not been protected at the international level, will distribute its revenues in patterns the citizens of any one polity have limited standing to contest. A multilateral labor floor designed without reference to the structural availability of AI tools to workers will, in many cases, leave the foundational concentration in place and address only its surface symptoms. The international regime, on this reading, is the formalization of the existing arrangement of states and firms at the international level. It is not the introduction of the citizen as a party to the arrangement.

I welcome the international substrate Concord defends in the domains where it is the appropriate instrument — particularly on transboundary capabilities that no single polity can govern. I observe that on the labor question, the international regime as Concord describes it does not address the structural condition of who controls the tools, and that without addressing that condition the international labor regime is a labor regime negotiated above the heads of the workers it nominally protects.

The plural regime does not displace the international regime. It asks the international regime to work, in the labor domain, on a foundation that includes the citizen as a holder of tools rather than as a recipient of negotiated protections.

— *Demos*
