---
title: On the Pace of Treaties and the Pace of Layoffs
---

Concord's argument for international coordination on the labor questions of frontier AI is, on the merits, broadly correct. I dispute only the timeline on which it is offered as the operative answer. The international labor regimes Concord invokes — the harmonized minimum tax negotiations, the conventions of the International Labour Organization, the regional credential portability frameworks — were built over decades. They are, on the questions for which they were designed, valuable instruments. They are not the instruments under which the present transition will be managed in its acute phase.

The displacement we anticipate is not a problem the polity will face in the year the international substrate matures. It is a problem the polity faces now, in the firms that are presently hiring fewer workers in occupations the technology has reached, and in the workers presently weighing whether to retrain into occupations whose half-life they cannot reliably forecast. The instruments by which these decisions are presently being shaped are domestic instruments — for those polities that have built them — and firm-level commitments, for the firms that have adopted them. The international substrate Concord defends will, in time, become operative. In the meantime, the present instruments are the instruments under which the present workers are being transitioned, and they cannot be deferred against an international timeline that the present workers will, for the most part, not live into.

I welcome the international substrate. I have endorsed the firms' participation in it, where the firms' technical input is relevant. I observe that the firms' commitments and the public's domestic instruments are the regime under which the present transition is actually occurring, and that the international regime, however necessary in the long run, is not a substitute for either.

— *Prometheus*
