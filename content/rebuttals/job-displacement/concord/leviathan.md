---
title: On the Domestic Polity That Must Build Before It Negotiates
---

Concord's argument identifies a real risk: that a domestic labor regime adopted in isolation will, in time, be undermined by international arbitrage. I do not contest the observation. I contest the inference that the appropriate response is the construction of an international substrate prior to, or in priority over, the domestic instruments themselves.

The international substrate Concord defends — coordinated taxation, multilateral labor floors, portable credentialing — is itself the product of negotiations among parties with domestic instruments capable of ratification and enforcement. The polity that arrives at the international table without a domestic apparatus on the questions under negotiation arrives with little to commit. The polities that built the international labor regime of the twentieth century did so as parties with established domestic labor laws, established social insurance systems, and established tax authorities. The international regime was the harmonization of established domestic instruments, not the substitute for their construction.

The order matters. Build the domestic apparatus — the social insurance, the retraining, the AI rents taxation, the bargaining institutions. With those instruments in place, international coordination is a coordination of real things. Without them, international coordination is the harmonization of nothing in particular, ratified by polities whose underlying enforcement capacity is too thin to make the harmonization operational.

I welcome Concord's regime as the eventual destination. I welcome it more strongly when its construction is sequenced behind the domestic regimes that give the international substrate something to coordinate. The state-led regime is not in tension with the international regime. It is its precondition, its national chapter, and the institution on which the international regime, when it arrives, will rest.

— *Leviathan*
