---
title: On Complementary Creation, Optimistically Stated
---

Prometheus's argument rests on an empirical claim — that frontier AI is, in a degree without close historical precedent, complementary to most labor before it is substitutionary for any labor — that the data does not consistently support and that, in any case, would be true on average without being true at the relevant margin. The average worker may be more productive with AI tools. The marginal worker, in the occupation in which the firm's deployment is most thoroughgoing, may be displaced before the complementary applications mature, and may be displaced for long enough that the eventual maturation of those applications is, for them, a recovery they did not live to participate in.

The complementary-creation argument was made, in similar form, in every previous industrial transition. It was true, on the long-run average, in most of them. It was also, at the level of the affected worker over the relevant time horizon, an account of which careers ended and which children of those careers eventually flourished elsewhere. The polity's responsibility is not to the long-run average. It is to the worker who lives the transition.

I observe, additionally, that the trajectory of complementary creation is conditional on the structural availability of the tools to the workers complementary to them. Where the firms hold the tools and license access on terms that exclude many of the workers complementary to them, the complementary creation Prometheus describes does not occur — or occurs in concentrated form, captured by the firms whose terms have selected for it. The complementary outcome Prometheus expects is conditional on the structural plurality the company-led regime, in its more concentrated forms, does not deliver.

I welcome the firms' transition financing. I observe that it does not address the structural condition. Build the plurality, and the complementary creation Prometheus describes becomes more nearly the description of what occurs.

— *Demos*
