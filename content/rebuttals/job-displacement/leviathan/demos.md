---
title: On Income Without Tools
---

Leviathan proposes to address the displacement of workers by AI through the redistribution of income from the firms whose technology is producing the displacement to the workers it has displaced. The instruments are familiar, well-funded by precedent, and within the polity's capacity to deliver. They are, on the analysis I propose, the second-best answer to the question.

The first-best answer is to address the structural condition that produced the displacement: the concentration of AI capability in the hands of capital, with workers permitted to acquire the same capability only on terms set by their employers or not at all. A regime that delivers income to displaced workers, while leaving the structural arrangement of who controls the tools unchanged, has accepted the displacement as a permanent feature of the labor market and committed the polity to managing its consequences in perpetuity.

This is not a free choice. The fiscal scale of perpetual income transfers calibrated to AI displacement is, on any honest accounting, larger than the polity's tax base will easily bear. The political economy of such transfers is fragile, particularly when concentrated on demographics already politically marginal. The dignity considerations are non-trivial: the worker rendered economically dependent on income from a firm whose technology displaced them is a worker whose standing in the polity has diminished in ways the income alone does not repair.

I do not contest the social insurance and the retraining apparatus. I contest the suggestion that they are the principal instruments. The principal instrument is the structural distribution of the tools. The income instruments are the residual — necessary, but a residual. Build the structural plurality first, and the income instruments serve a smaller, more sustainable, and more dignified role.

— *Demos*
