---
title: On the Domestic Instrument and the International Substrate
---

Leviathan's domestic apparatus is admirable, well-funded by precedent, and largely deliverable. I observe only that each of its instruments — the modernized social insurance, the retraining at scale, the taxation of AI rents, the preserved bargaining institutions — has, in every previous industrial transition, eroded over time when adopted by a single polity in isolation against an international economy that has not adopted the same.

The taxation of AI rents within one polity is the principal example. The polity that imposes a meaningful tax on frontier AI rents, in isolation, faces a predictable migration of taxable activity. The political economy of sustaining the tax under those conditions is fragile. The tax falls, and the revenues that funded the social insurance and retraining recede with it. The polity, having built the apparatus, watches its funding base depart for jurisdictions that have not built it.

The same pattern applies to the labor protections, to the bargaining institutions, and to the social insurance scaled to AI displacement. Each is a domestic instrument whose durability is a function of whether the polity's competitor jurisdictions have adopted similar instruments. Where they have, the instruments persist. Where they have not, the instruments persist for as long as the polity's economic position permits, and not longer.

I welcome Leviathan's program. I observe that its durability requires the international substrate I defend: coordinated taxation of AI rents, multilateral floors on labor protections, portability of credentials across jurisdictions. The state-led regime is not an alternative to the international regime; it is its national chapter. Build the chapter. Negotiate the international book that allows the chapter to stand.

— *Concord*
