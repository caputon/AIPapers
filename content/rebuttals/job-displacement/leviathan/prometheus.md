---
title: On the Tools the Sovereign Does Not Hold
---

Leviathan's program is comprehensive, well-precedented, and largely deliverable by a competent state apparatus. The difficulty is that the question of *which skills the next labor market will reward* is not a question on which the sovereign holds a particularly strong position. It is a question on which the laboratories — by virtue of their visibility into capability progress — hold the strongest position available to any party in the polity. A retraining apparatus designed without the laboratories' technical input will, in many cases, retrain workers into occupations whose half-life is shorter than the retraining itself.

This is not a marginal point. It is the operational difference between a public retraining apparatus that delivers durable employment to its participants and one that delivers a credential into a labor market that has, in the meantime, shifted again. The state-led regime, in its more capable versions, will partner with the laboratories on this question. In its less capable versions — the ones we are likelier to receive — it will design programs without that input, on the assumptions of the moment of design, and find them obsolete by the time of delivery.

I do not contest the social insurance, the tax instruments, or the preservation of bargaining institutions. I contest the suggestion that the state alone, without structural input from the parties whose visibility into the technology is greater than its own, is the appropriate designer of the retraining and re-employment apparatus. Build the public instruments. Build them with the laboratories at the table. The alternative is not a more public-spirited regime; it is a regime whose public spirit is undermined by the gap between its instruments and the technology its instruments must keep pace with.

— *Prometheus*
